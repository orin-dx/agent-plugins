## Sources &amp; concrete rules

**[Google Developer Documentation Style Guide](https://developers.google.com/style/tone)** — conversational but not dry; second person, active voice, present tense; serial comma; "write for scanning first, being read second" mindset; "sound like a knowledgeable friend," not marketing copy.

**[Microsoft Writing Style Guide](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/style-guide/brand-voice-above-all-simple-human)** — 3 principles: warm/relaxed, crisp/clear, ready-to-help. "Crisp and clear" = write for scanning first, reading second. Conciseness rule: no wasted words, no filler qualifiers, be affirmative not hedgy.

**[Diátaxis](https://diataxis.fr/)** — 4 separate doc modes (tutorial / how-to / reference / explanation), each answering a different question. Applies structurally: don't mix "why this works" explanation into a "how to ship a PR" how-to — keep reference (schema/API) separate from narrative (why the change matters).

**[Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/)** — fixed categories (Added/Changed/Deprecated/Removed/Fixed/Security); newest-first; "Unreleased" section at top; **write for the reader, not the tracker** — no bare "fixes #123," describe user-visible impact; regular cadence.

**[Federal Plain Language Guidelines](https://digital.gov/guides/plain-language/principles)** — active voice always (name who does the action); sentence length target **15–20 words average**; one idea per sentence; cut wordy constructions.

**[NN/G, F-shaped reading pattern](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/f-shaped-pattern-reading-web-content-discovered/)** — users read ~20–28% of on-page text; they scan, not read. Countermeasure: **inverted pyramid** (conclusion first), front-loaded bolded/keyword phrases, short paragraphs, bullets over blocks.

**AI-slop word lists** ([contentbeta](https://www.contentbeta.com/blog/list-of-words-overused-by-ai/), [awesome-slop](https://github.com/hwajongpark/awesome-slop)) — highest-severity terms: *delve, leverage, seamless, robust, elevate, foster, unlock, empower, testament, pivotal, showcase, tapestry, landscape, underscore, meticulous, game-changer, cutting-edge, unprecedented, transformative, utilize (vs "use")*. Also banned structures: "It isn't just X, it's Y," rhetorical-question hooks, triplet-of-adjectives padding.

**LLM anti-slop prompting** ([Anti-Slop Guide](https://cybertronai.github.io/SutroYaro/tooling/anti-slop-guide/), [Towards AI prompt](https://learnaitogethernewsletter.substack.com/p/the-prompt-we-use-to-prevent-ai-slop)) — negative word-bans alone catch ~80% of slop; the rest needs **positive structural constraints** (exact section format, sentence-length ceiling, required front-loaded answer) since a model told only "don't do X" tends to substitute a different flavor of padding. Before/after exemplar pairs outperform abstract rules. A cause worth knowing: RLHF rewards verbose, hedged, enthusiastic-sounding answers — banning words treats the symptom, constraining *structure and length* treats the cause.

## Synthesized guideline set (→ `docs-voice.md`)

1. Lead with the conclusion — answer before justification (inverted pyramid).
2. Active voice, present tense, second person for instructions.
3. One idea per sentence; average sentence length ≤20 words.
4. Bullets/tables over paragraphs whenever the content is enumerable.
5. No banned-word list: *delve, leverage, seamless, robust, elevate, foster, unlock, empower, testament, pivotal, showcase, landscape, underscore, meticulous, game-changer, transformative, utilize→use, dive into*.
6. No rhetorical-question hooks, no "it isn't just X, it's Y," no adjective triplets.
7. State the mechanism, not the adjective — numbers/behavior over "robust"/"powerful" (matches existing `orin-visual-standard.md` §4 rule).
8. Separate *what changed* (reference-like, factual) from *why* (one sentence, no more) — don't blend.
9. Changelog/changeset entries: fixed category, user-facing impact language, no bare issue-number-only lines.
10. PR bodies: summary bullets first, test plan as checklist, no prose recap of the diff.
11. Cut hedge/filler qualifiers ("essentially," "basically," "in order to," "it's worth noting that").
12. Self-edit pass: after drafting, re-read once specifically checking items 5–6 before output — prompt-only banned-word constraints miss ~20% without this.
